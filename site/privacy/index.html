Privacy

Herdr Anywhere connects your phone to the computer you pair it with. We run no server and never see what passes between them.

  • Your terminals, your agents and what you type into them go between your phone and your own computer, through the herdr-anywhere companion you run there.
  • Paired through Tailcat, Tailscale’s open-source tunnel, which runs in the app and on your computer, the connection is encrypted end to end with WireGuard. Tailcat’s public relay servers help the two devices find each other and carry the traffic when a direct connection is not possible. They see device keys and IP addresses, not your terminals. The app gets the list of relays from tailcat.dev.
  • Paired over your local network instead, the connection is not encrypted, and anyone on that network can read it.
  • On your phone, the app keeps the pairing with your computer, your settings, and your last view of its spaces and agents. Forget computer in Settings removes the pairing.
  • On your computer, the companion keeps a list of paired phones with their names and when each last connected, and a log of its connections. It stores pairing codes and phone tokens only as hashes.
  • The companion asks GitHub about new releases once a day, only after you said yes to that in the app or turned on automatic updates, and downloads one when you or a paired phone ask, or by itself if you turned on automatic updates. The app reaches GitHub only to download a new version after you tap Download. GitHub sees the IP address of the device asking.
  • The camera is used only to scan the pairing QR code, and no picture is saved.
  • Try it runs on sample data in the app and connects to nothing.
  • The app has no analytics, no advertising identifiers and no third-party SDKs that phone home.
  • In the TestFlight beta, Apple collects crash reports, usage data and the feedback you choose to send and shares them with us, under Apple’s TestFlight terms.
